State of nature
War of all against all — violent, fearful, without justice
Inconvenient but peaceful — rights exist, but no enforcement
Natural goodness and equality — corruption came later, with society
Why form society
To escape mutual destruction; survival demands a sovereign
To protect pre-existing natural rights more reliably
We shouldn't have — private property and inequality made it inevitable
Basis of authority
Power surrendered to sovereign — irrevocably, for security
Consent of the governed; rights are retained, not surrendered
The general will — the collective good of the whole people
If government fails
Irrelevant — you already surrendered your rights; chaos is worse
Revolution is justified — government has broken the contract
It was probably corrupt from the start; reform toward the general will
Feeds into
Authoritarianism, strong-state conservatism
Liberalism, constitutionalism, the American founding
Democracy, romanticism, revolutionary and socialist politics
